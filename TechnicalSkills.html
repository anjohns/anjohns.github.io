Technical Skills and Experience

Statistical Analysis and Machine Learning

I use R and Python for statistical analysis, modeling, and visualization. My experience includes linear and generalized linear models, mixed-effects models, distribution fitting, and hypothesis testing. I also work extensively with tree-based machine learning methods, including XGBoost, LightGBM, and random forests. I've developed pipelines that combine these algorithms into stacked ensembles and use feature importance methods to interpret their predictions. In my recent New York City research, these approaches reduced prediction error by 38–50% relative to a simple baseline. I try to understand what a model has learned, where it performs poorly, and what its results can reasonably tell us.

Spatial Analysis and Computational Simulation

Much of my recent work involves large geospatial datasets and models of human movement. I've worked with millions of anonymized mobility observations, street-network geometries, land-use data, and points of interest. My experience includes spatial joins, network analysis, feature engineering, and modeling spatial dependence. I've also developed agent-based simulations in Python to study how people navigate street networks, including comparisons between locally determined routes and shortest paths. I'm particularly interested in how relatively simple individual decisions can produce complicated patterns at larger scales.

Programming and Data Workflows

Python is my primary programming language, followed by R. I also have experience with SQL and Java, and regularly use Jupyter, Git, and GitHub. My projects often require building datasets from sources that weren't designed to work together. I've developed workflows for cleaning and joining spatial data, extracting structured information from documents, and automating repetitive tasks. These include web-scraping applications, API integrations, and automated reporting tools. I enjoy building tools that make difficult or tedious work easier to repeat.

Large Language Models and AI Applications

I've developed applications using both locally hosted language models and commercial APIs. One of my larger independent projects is a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system built from a web-scraped and cleaned corpus of published fiction. I designed a two-stage retrieval process that combines semantic vector search with reranking, then integrated the system into LM Studio so it can be used directly through the chat interface. I've also built automated applications using the OpenAI API, including a program that retrieves and summarizes presidential actions.

I've become particularly interested in the architecture and practical limitations of local language models. I've worked with quantized models, sparse mixture-of-experts architectures, and different inference engines. One recent experiment involved running a 75.8 GB Qwen model on a desktop with 64 GB of RAM and 12 GB of VRAM, using Strata and the model's SSD-backed n-gram embeddings to work around memory limitations. This has given me practical experience with the relationship between model architecture, hardware constraints, and inference performance.

Experimental Design

My scientific training is rooted in designing controlled experiments to answer ecological and microbiological questions. During my Ph.D., I developed a Drosophila-based experimental system for studying the assembly and stability of gut microbial communities. This required careful control of experimental conditions and methods that could be replicated across many trials. That training continues to shape how I approach computational research. I pay close attention to assumptions, confounding variables, and the distinction between finding a pattern and explaining its cause.

Writing

I write for scientific, policy, and general audiences. My academic work includes peer-reviewed publications and a dissertation on microbial community ecology. I've also written policy essays on biodefense, pandemic risk, and scientific research with potential security implications. I enjoy making technical ideas understandable without sacrificing their complexity. Outside of scientific writing, I write fiction and creative nonfiction, with work published in River Teeth.

Speaking and Scientific Communication

I've presented research to scientists, economists, policy specialists, and interdisciplinary advisory groups. My talks have covered subjects ranging from microbiome ecology to urban mobility and machine learning. I've presented at scientific conferences and communicated technical findings to audiences with little background in the methods being used. I spend a great deal of time thinking about how to explain an idea clearly, particularly when the audience approaches the problem from another field.

Research Leadership and Mentorship

I've led interdisciplinary research teams of up to six people and mentored 14 trainees at different stages of their education. My experience includes developing research questions, coordinating technical work, troubleshooting experiments, and helping collaborators bring projects to completion. I've worked with people from a wide range of scientific backgrounds and enjoy finding ways to make their different expertise useful to a shared problem.
